# The Amazon Behavioral Interview

Amazon has no single behavioral round. Every interviewer in the loop asks behavioral questions, and each question is built on one of the 16 Leadership Principles. The Leadership Principles are the written statements that describe how Amazon expects its employees to decide and act.

This page explains how the principles are used in the loop. It does not explain each principle. The free [Amazon Leadership Principles](https://github.com/design-gurus/amazon-leadership-principles) guide does that, with one page for each principle.

## How the principles are used

| Fact, as commonly reported | What it means for the candidate |
|---|---|
| Each interviewer is assigned two or three principles in advance | The loop as a whole covers most of the 16 principles |
| Behavioral questions take 10 to 25 minutes of each technical interview | Stories must be about two minutes long |
| Interviewers write down what the candidate says | Specific sentences and numbers are the evidence |
| Each principle receives a rating from Strength to Concern | One clear Concern is difficult to overcome in the debrief |
| Interviewers compare notes in the debrief | Repeating one story across interviews weakens the evidence |

## The Bar Raiser interview

A Bar Raiser is a trained interviewer from outside the hiring team. The hiring manager needs the role filled, and the Bar Raiser does not, which is the reason the role exists. Three facts are commonly reported:

- The Bar Raiser checks that the candidate is stronger than half of the people already in similar roles.
- The Bar Raiser can veto a hire.
- The interview is mostly behavioral, and the follow-up questions build on earlier answers.

Typical follow-up questions ask what the numbers were, what the other person said, and what the candidate would change.

## Question themes

| Theme | Example question | Principle usually tested |
|---|---|---|
| Decisions with incomplete data | Tell me about a decision you made without all the data you wanted. | Are Right, A Lot |
| Disagreement | Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager. | Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit |
| Difficult problems | Describe the hardest problem you have solved. | Dive Deep |
| Customers | Tell me about a time you changed a plan because of customer feedback. | Customer Obsession |
| Deadlines | Tell me about a time you delivered a project under a tight deadline. | Deliver Results |
| Motivation | Why do you want to work at Amazon? | No single principle |

## How to prepare stories

Answer with the STAR method. STAR means Situation, Task, Action, and Result: the context, your responsibility, the steps you took, and the measured outcome.

1. Write 10 to 12 stories from your own work. Each story should take about two minutes to tell.
2. Map each story to two principles, so that one story can answer several questions.
3. Put a number in every result, such as a latency, a cost, or a count of customers.
4. Say "I" for your own actions. A story about what the team did gives the interviewer no evidence about you.
5. Prepare each story two or three follow-up questions deep.
6. Write the answer to "Why Amazon?" in advance, because it is reported in almost every loop.
