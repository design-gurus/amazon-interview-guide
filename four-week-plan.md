# A Four-Week Plan for the Amazon Interview

Four weeks at 10 to 12 hours per week is a reasonable plan for an engineer who already writes code every day. The plan starts the behavioral stories in week 1, because every Amazon interview contains behavioral questions. Each week ends with a finish condition, which is a test that shows the week's work is complete.

## The weekly division of time

| Week | Coding | System design | Behavioral | Main goal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 hours | 1 hour | 4 hours | Pass the online assessment and draft the stories |
| 2 | 5 hours | 3 hours | 3 hours | Solve medium problems in 30 minutes |
| 3 | 3 hours | 5 hours | 3 hours | Complete a design answer in 35 minutes |
| 4 | 3 hours | 3 hours | 5 hours | Rehearse full interviews |

A candidate for SDE I can move the design hours to coding and to object-oriented design, which means designing the classes of one small program. A candidate for SDE III should move two coding hours per week to design.

## Week 1: the assessment patterns and the first stories

- Practice arrays, strings, hash maps, and sliding window. Solve the four easy problems in [Grokking Amazon Coding Interview](https://www.designgurus.io/course/grokking-amazon-coding-interview?utm_source=github&utm_medium=repo&utm_campaign=amazon-interview-guide&utm_content=four-week-plan).
- Take one timed set of two problems in 70 minutes, with the last 10 minutes for testing.
- Read the 16 Leadership Principles, the statements that describe how Amazon expects employees to act.
- Draft six stories in the STAR form, which means Situation, Task, Action, and Result.

**Finish condition:** two problems solved in 70 minutes with code that compiles, and six stories written with a number in each result.

## Week 2: medium problems and the design method

- Practice heaps, graph search, intervals, and topological sort. Solve the six medium problems in the course.
- Say the approach and the cost aloud for every problem.
- Learn one design method and use it on a URL shortening service.
- Add four stories, and map every story to two principles.

**Finish condition:** three medium problems in a row solved in 30 minutes each, and 10 stories mapped to at least 12 principles.

## Week 3: design on Amazon products

- Work through four designs: a shopping cart, a storage service, a video streaming service, and a key-value database.
- For each design, write the request rate, the storage per year, and two failure cases.
- State one cost decision in every design.
- Solve the four hard problems in the course, with no time limit on the first attempt.
- Answer three follow-up questions for each story: the numbers, the other person's view, and what you would change.

**Finish condition:** one design completed in 35 minutes with numbers and failure cases, and every story answered three questions deep.

## Week 4: full interviews

- Run four practice interviews of 60 minutes with another person. Each one starts with two behavioral questions and continues with one coding or design question.
- Write and rehearse the answer to "Why Amazon?"
- Repeat every problem that took more than 30 minutes.
- Use a different story in each practice interview, as the loop requires.
- Ask the recruiter for the target level and the list of interviews.

**Finish condition:** four practice interviews completed, each with a working solution or a complete design, and no story used twice.

## The day before

1. Read the story list once and do not add new stories.
2. Test the camera, the microphone, and the shared editor.
3. Prepare two questions to ask each interviewer.
