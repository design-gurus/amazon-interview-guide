# The Amazon Interview Process

The Amazon software engineer process is commonly reported to have five to seven stages over three to six weeks. The main stages are an application review, an online assessment, a phone screen, and a set of interviews called the loop. A meeting called the debrief then decides the result.

Amazon does not publish every detail, and teams differ. Treat the numbers below as typical, and follow the recruiter's email when it says something different.

## The stages in order

| Stage | Commonly reported format | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Application review | A recruiter reads the resume against the role | Whether the basic qualifications are met |
| 2. Online assessment | A timed test in a browser, about 90 minutes to 2 hours | Whether an engineer will spend time on an interview |
| 3. Phone screen | 30 to 60 minutes with a recruiter, an engineer, or the hiring manager | Whether to schedule the loop |
| 4. Loop | Four to six interviews of 45 to 60 minutes each | The evidence for the hiring decision |
| 5. Debrief | The interviewers meet and compare written feedback | Hire or no hire, and the level |
| 6. Offer | A recruiter call, then a background check | Terms and start date |

## The online assessment

The online assessment (OA) is an unproctored test, which means no person watches the candidate take it. It is sent after the application passes the first review, and candidates are commonly reported to have seven days to complete it.

| Section | New graduate | Intern | Experienced SDE II |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coding, two questions | About 70 minutes | About 70 minutes | About 90 minutes |
| Workstyles survey | About 15 minutes | About 15 minutes | About 10 minutes |
| Work Simulation | About 60 minutes | Not included | Not included |
| System design questions | Not included | Not included | About 15 minutes |

Three facts about the OA are commonly reported:

- Each section is judged separately, and a candidate must pass every section.
- The code must be syntactically correct. Pseudo code, which is a plain-language description of an algorithm that does not compile, is not accepted.
- The Workstyles survey and the Work Simulation are judged against the Leadership Principles, the 16 statements that describe how Amazon expects employees to act.

## The phone screen

Technical candidates usually receive one coding question in a shared editor, together with a few behavioral questions. Explain the plan before typing, and write working code in a real language. Candidates are told to expect a result within about two business days.

## The loop

The loop is the final stage. Each interview is a separate meeting with one Amazon employee.

| Interview | Usual count | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Coding | 2 | One problem in a shared editor, plus 10 to 15 minutes of behavioral questions |
| System design | 1 or 2 for SDE II and above | One open design question, plus behavioral questions |
| Bar Raiser | 1 | Mostly behavioral questions with many follow-up questions |
| Hiring manager | Often 1 | Background, motivation, and behavioral questions |

Each interviewer is commonly reported to be assigned two or three Leadership Principles in advance. The Bar Raiser is a trained interviewer from another team. Candidates are not told which interviewer holds that role, so every interview deserves the same preparation.

## The debrief and the result

Every interviewer writes feedback and records a vote before the group meets. The reported votes are Strong Hire, Inclined Hire, Inclined No Hire, and Strong No Hire. The group then discusses the written evidence, and the Bar Raiser usually leads the meeting.

- The debrief is commonly reported to happen within one to three days of the loop.
- Candidates are told to expect a decision within about five business days.
- A rejection is usually a short message without detailed feedback.
- Amazon publishes no fixed waiting period for a new application. Candidates commonly report six months to one year.

Some candidates are also offered a Candid Chat, an optional and informal conversation with an Amazon employee that is not an interview.
