# Amazon SDE Levels and the Interview

Amazon software development engineer (SDE) levels run from L4 to L8, and most external candidates interview for L4, L5, or L6. The interviews have the same structure at every level. The weight of each interview changes: coding matters most at L4, and system design and the size of the behavioral stories matter most from L6 upward.

These levels are commonly reported by employees and candidates. They are not an official Amazon publication.

## The ladder

| Internal level | Title | Typical scope |
|---|---|---|
| **L4** | SDE I | Well-defined tasks inside one team, with regular review |
| **L5** | SDE II | Features built end to end with little supervision |
| **L6** | SDE III (Senior SDE) | A project or problem area owned for one team |
| **L7** | Principal Engineer | Work that spans several teams or a whole organization |
| **L8** | Senior Principal Engineer | Direction across a whole product line or division |

Three facts help when reading the table:

- Amazon uses one level system for the whole company. L1 to L3 belong to warehouse, fulfillment, and support roles, so engineering starts at L4.
- L5 is the terminal level, which means an engineer can stay at that level for a whole career without pressure to be promoted.
- Amazon has no staff title. L7 Principal Engineer is the closest match to the staff level at other large companies.

## How the interview changes by level

| Level | Coding | Design | Behavioral stories |
|---|---|---|---|
| L4 (SDE I) | The main evidence. The online assessment includes a Work Simulation for new graduates. | Often object-oriented design, which means designing the classes of one small program | Stories from school projects and internships are accepted |
| L5 (SDE II) | Two interviews are commonly reported | One system design interview. The online assessment adds short design questions. | A feature owned from start to finish |
| L6 (SDE III) | Still asked, and rarely decides the level | One or two interviews. These usually decide the level. | A project led for a team, with influence on other teams |
| L7 (Principal Engineer) | A smaller share of the evidence | Design across several teams | Direction set for an organization, with a record at that scope |

Every interviewer is commonly reported to assess Leadership Principles at every level. The Leadership Principles are the 16 statements that describe how Amazon expects employees to act. A higher level does not remove the behavioral questions. It raises the scope that each story must show.

## How the level is decided

The level is set from the loop. Interviewers are commonly reported to be calibrated for a target level before the loop starts, and the debrief confirms or lowers it. The L5 band is wide, so a candidate who could be hired at L6 may receive an L5 offer. Three actions reduce that risk:

1. Ask the recruiter for the target level before the loop is scheduled.
2. Name any competing offer by its level, because the target is rarely changed after the loop.
3. Tell behavioral stories at the scope of the level you want. For L6, that means influence beyond your own team.

An external hire at L7 is commonly reported to need a record of work at that scope at a previous employer.

## Preparing by level

- **L4:** spend most of the time on coding patterns and on the online assessment. Add one object-oriented design problem each week.
- **L5:** divide the time evenly between coding, system design, and stories.
- **L6 and L7:** spend more time on system design than on coding. Rewrite each story so that it states the number of teams and the measured result.
