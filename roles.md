# Amazon Interviews by Role

The loop described in this guide is the one for a software development engineer (SDE). Interns and new graduates receive a shorter version with a longer online assessment. An AWS solutions architect receives a different final stage that includes a presentation. The Leadership Principles, the 16 statements that describe how Amazon expects employees to act, are used for every role.

This page covers only the roles that the source articles describe. For any other role, ask the recruiter for the list of interviews.

## SDE versions compared

| Item | Intern | New graduate (SDE I) | Experienced (SDE II and above) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Online assessment coding | Two questions, about 70 minutes | Two questions, about 70 minutes | Two questions, about 90 minutes |
| Work Simulation | Not included | About 60 minutes | Not included |
| Design questions in the assessment | Not included | Not included | About 15 minutes of short questions |
| Design in the interviews | Rare | Often object-oriented design | System design, one or two interviews |
| Behavioral stories | School and project work | School, projects, and internships | Features and projects owned at work |

Object-oriented design means designing the classes and methods of one small program. The Work Simulation is a set of short situations from a working day. The candidate chooses a response or ranks several responses, and the answers are judged against the Leadership Principles. Candidates report that failing this section fails the whole assessment, whatever the coding result.

## AWS solutions architect

A solutions architect (SA) is a technical expert who designs a customer's solution on AWS and stays involved until it runs in production. AWS does not publish its SA loop, so the stages below come from a small number of reports that do not fully agree.

| Stage | Commonly reported format | What is evaluated |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiter call | A call with a recruiter | Background and fit for the role |
| Online assessment | Optional, with technical and behavioral sections | Technical knowledge and work judgment |
| Phone screen | One interviewer, mostly technical, with one or two behavioral questions | Breadth on core cloud services |
| Final loop | Five interviews in one report, four to six in another | Leadership Principles, system design, and depth in one area |
| Presentation | 20 minutes plus 10 minutes of questions in one report, 30 minutes in another | Communication and technical judgment |

Four differences from the SDE loop are reported:

- **No algorithm coding is described.** The technical interviews are a cloud design for a customer need and a depth interview in the candidate's specialty.
- **The requirements change during the design.** The interviewer changes a requirement, and the candidate must adjust the design and explain the new trade-off.
- **Behavioral questions take a larger share.** One report describes three mostly behavioral interviews out of five.
- **There is a presentation.** The candidate presents one past project, with a clear problem, a rejected option, and a measured result.

The reports do not say whether an SA loop includes a Bar Raiser, the trained interviewer from outside the hiring team. Ask the recruiter, and prepare every behavioral answer to the same standard.

## What stays the same in every role

1. Questions are based on the Leadership Principles.
2. Answers are expected in the STAR form, which means Situation, Task, Action, and Result.
3. Interviewers ask for the candidate's own decisions and the candidate's own numbers.
